1. **Alex — A First-Time National Park Visitor**

Alex is a college student who wants to visit a national park in the United States for the first time. He wants to use the website to explore park locations on a map and find a destination for a weekend trip. He needs clear park introductions, photos, and filters for easy hiking trails and basic facilities so he can compare his options. Alex has little outdoor experience and limited experience with mapping tools, so he prefers a simple interface with easy-to-understand labels.

2. **Sarah — A Parent Planning a Family Trip**

Sarah is a mother of two young children who wants to plan a family vacation to a national park. She wants to use the website to find parks with short, easy trails and convenient facilities. She needs filters for family-friendly activities, restrooms, picnic areas, and visitor centers, along with information about trail length and temperature. Sarah is comfortable using travel websites but has limited hiking experience, so she prefers practical information that helps her judge whether a park is suitable for her children.

3. **Michael — An Experienced Hiker**

Michael is an experienced hiker who enjoys challenging outdoor adventures. He wants to use the website to discover national parks with difficult trails and varied landscapes. He needs trail information such as distance, elevation gain, estimated hiking time, and seasonal conditions to choose routes that match his abilities. Michael is familiar with digital maps and hiking terminology, so he prefers detailed information and filters that let him narrow down his choices efficiently.

4. **Emily — A Dog Owner**

Emily is a dog owner who wants to include her pet in a national park trip. She wants to use the website to identify parks with pet-friendly areas and compare the activities she can enjoy with her dog. She needs a pet-friendly filter and specific information about where dogs are allowed, leash requirements, and restrictions on individual trails. Emily is comfortable browsing websites but unfamiliar with national park pet policies, so she prefers clear explanations that help her understand the limits of each park’s pet-friendly label.

5. **David — A Road Trip Traveler**

David is a road trip enthusiast who wants to visit several national parks during a driving vacation. He wants to use the map to understand where parks are located and identify stops along his planned route. He needs filters for scenic drives and vehicle access, along with information about park entrances, parking, and seasonal road closures. David is confident using navigation apps but knows little about individual national parks, so he prefers a clear geographic overview with accessible driving information.

6. **Linda — An Older Traveler Seeking Gentle Activities**

Linda is a retired teacher who enjoys nature and prefers activities that require limited physical effort. She needs information about trail surfaces, slopes, benches, restrooms, and viewpoints accessible by car or shuttle. Linda has basic computer skills and little experience with interactive maps, so she prefers readable text, clear navigation, and filters that are easy to select.

Olivia has strong photography skills but moderate hiking experience, so she prefers visual browsing supported by clear information about the effort required to reach a location.
